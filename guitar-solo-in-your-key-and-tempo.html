Is there an app that writes a guitar solo in the key and tempo you choose?

Updated 29 October 2026

Yes. FretShed, for iPhone, iPad and Mac, generates a 30-second guitar solo over a backing track in the genre, key and tempo you pick: rock, blues, jazz, metal or funk, any major or minor key, 60 to 120 bpm. There are no tabs: you learn it by ear, loop the hard phrases and slow them down without changing the pitch.

It is built for intermediate and advanced players at two moments. When there's a gig on Friday, the song needs a solo and you don't have one yet. And when you keep reaching for the same licks and your ear needs something it hasn't heard.

What you get

  • A solo in the genre, key and tempo you set, 30 seconds long.
  • A backing track under it, in the same key and tempo.
  • A fresh solo every time. You will not hear the same thing twice.
  • No tabs and no notation. If you can see it, you're reading it, not hearing it.
  • A loop you drag across any phrase, and playback down to 50% speed with the pitch where it is.

FretShed uses AI to generate a solo. You work it out. You make it yours.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't follow chord changes. It matches genre, key and tempo. It won't trace the progression of a particular song, so expect to change a few notes to fit the changes.
  • It doesn't give you tab. That is deliberate. A solo you work out by ear stays in your hands.
  • It doesn't teach lessons. There are no lessons, no gamification, no badges. It is not for beginners.

How to work one up for a gig

  1. Set the key and tempo. Use the song's key and tempo, and the genre closest to its feel.
  2. Listen before you play. Generate a solo and listen through twice before you pick up the guitar. Hear where it breathes.
  3. Loop the first phrase. Find its first note by ear, then the shape of the line.
  4. Slow it down. Drop the speed until you can play the phrase cleanly, as low as 50%. The pitch stays where it is. Push the speed back up to tempo.
  5. Join the phrases. Move the loop to the next phrase. When two phrases are under your fingers, loop them together.
  6. Make it fit. Play it against the song, not only the backing track. Change the feel, move or extend the ending, and change any note that clashes with the song's chords.

If a solo doesn't suit the song after the first listen, generate another. Each one you work out adds to your vocabulary, and you need the next one a little less.

Other kinds of tool, and when they fit better

  • Chord-based arrangers. Band-in-a-Box, on Windows and Mac, builds an arrangement over chords you type in. If every note has to follow a song's exact changes, start there.
  • Web tools that turn a prompt into tab. They give you something to read. FretShed gives you something to hear.
  • Backing-track apps. They give you the band in any key, and you improvise over it. Good when you want to make a solo up yourself from nothing.
  • Slow-down apps such as Anytune and Transcribe!. They slow a recording you already own, so they fit when the solo you need is on someone else's record.

Where FretShed fits

FretShed is for the gap between those: you need a solo in a set key and tempo, there isn't one to copy, and you want to learn it by ear rather than read it. The recorder, metronome and ear tuner are free. A monthly or yearly subscription unlocks solo generation, up to 80 solos in any 30 days, on iPhone, iPad and Mac.
